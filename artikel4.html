Adaptation Process

By SiripJuara

January 06, 2026 | 17.42 WITA

Moving to a new container is the most vulnerable moment for a betta fish. Differences in temperature, water quality, and environment can trigger stress that leads to a decline in health — or even death if the fish goes into shock.

The adaptation process should be done gradually. When moving the fish for the first time, don't immediately place it into the new container. Keep the fish in its original bag or container, then float it in the new water for 15–30 minutes so the temperature can slowly adjust.

After that, gradually mix small amounts of water from the new container into the old one every 5–10 minutes. Repeat this several times until the water volume increases and the conditions start to match. This is important to avoid sudden differences in pH and dissolved substances that can shock the fish.

Use a net when transferring the fish to the new container — don't pour in all the old water. Water from the original source often contains waste or bacteria that can actually harm the new environment.

The initial environment should be kept "calm." Avoid bright lights, loud noises, or vibrations. Don't feed the fish right away — wait a few hours until the fish appears more stable. Feeding too soon can make the stress even worse.

During the first 1–2 days, focus on observation. A fish that is adapting well will start swimming normally, spreading its fins, and responding to movement around it. If the fish appears pale, stays at the bottom, or gasps at the surface, that's a sign of a problem — usually from water quality or an unsuitable temperature.

Signs of successful adaptation include the fish actively swimming, being responsive, and eating willingly. If the fish stays at the bottom or appears lethargic, it may still be stressed or there may be an issue with the water conditions.
