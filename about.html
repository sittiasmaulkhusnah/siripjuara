History of Betta Fish

The name "Betta" comes from
an ancient warrior clan,
reflecting the fish’s aggressive
and territorial nature.

In Thailand (formerly Siam) during
the 19th century, betta fish were
highly popular as fighting fish.
Their matches were even
regulated through official licenses
and taxation by the King of Siam,
earning them the nickname
"Siamese Fighting Fish."

In the late 1800s, betta fish
were finally introduced to the Western
world.

Different Types of Betta Fish

Halfmoon Series ˅

Includes: Halfmoon, Halfmoon Blue, Rosetail, Rosetail Blue

Halfmoon betta fish are the result of modern breeding. They are among the most popular types and are often considered part of the premium category.

Characteristics: 180° tail spread, full and symmetrical fins.

Double Tail ˅

Includes: Halfmoon, Halfmoon Blue, Rosetail, Rosetail Blue

Double Tail betta have two distinct tail lobes, giving them a dramatic, symmetrical appearance. This trait comes from a genetic mutation affecting fin development.

Characteristics:180° tail spread, full and symmetrical fins.

Plakat ˅

Includes: Halfmoon, Halfmoon Blue, Rosetail, Rosetail Blue

Plakat bettas are the closest to wild betta fish, with shorter fins that make them agile swimmers. They are strong and less prone to fin damage.

Characteristics: 180° tail spread, full and symmetrical fins.

Crown Tail ˅

Includes: Halfmoon, Halfmoon Blue, Rosetail, Rosetail Blue

Crown Tail bettas are known for their dramatic spiked fins that resemble a crown. The webbing between the fin rays is reduced, creating a striking spiky appearance.

Characteristics: Spiked fins with reduced webbing, dramatic silhouette.
