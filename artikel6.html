Understanding Betta Behavior

By SiripJuara

August 17, 2025 | 10.55 WITA

Understanding the character and behavior of betta fish is important so that owners can better assess the fish's condition. The behaviors displayed by betta fish can be signs of whether the fish is healthy, stressed, or sick. By understanding their natural behavior, owners can create a more comfortable environment so that betta fish can live more actively and healthily.

  1. Territorial & Aggressive Nature

Betta fish are territorial by nature, especially males. It is therefore not recommended to mix multiple male bettas in one container, as this can lead to fighting and serious injuries.

  1. Flaring Behavior

Bettas often display flaring behavior — spreading their fins and gill covers when they see another fish or their own reflection. This is a natural behavior, but it should not happen too frequently as it can stress the fish. Limit mirror exposure to a few minutes per day at most.

  1. Resting Habits

Betta fish often rest on plant leaves, in the corners of the container, or near the water surface. This behavior is completely normal for betta fish and is not a sign of illness unless accompanied by other symptoms.

  1. Surface Breathing

Betta fish have a labyrinth organ that allows them to take oxygen directly from the air. Because of this, they are often seen rising to the surface. This is normal — however, constant gasping at the surface may indicate low oxygen or gill problems.

  1. Signs of a Healthy Betta

A healthy betta fish is usually actively swimming, has vibrant body colors, open fins, and is responsive to its surroundings. It will eat eagerly and explore its environment regularly.

  1. Signs of Stress or Illness

A fish that stays still for long periods, has faded colors, clamped fins, or shows loss of appetite may be stressed or sick. These signs should prompt an immediate check of water quality and environment.

  1. Territory Patrolling Habit

Betta fish typically swim around their container as a way of patrolling their territory. This is a natural behavior and indicates the fish is comfortable and active in its environment.
