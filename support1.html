Do not place the fish directly into the tank. First, float the plastic bag for around 15–30 minutes so the water temperature can adjust. Then, slowly add a small amount of tank water into the bag so the fish can adapt. After that, transfer the fish into the new tank without mixing too much of the old water. It is also recommended not to feed the fish immediately to avoid stress.
Betta fish colors can stay bright when fed nutritious food such as mosquito larvae or special pellets, with regular water changes and the addition of catappa leaves. Avoid stressing the fish by not moving it too often, so it stays more stable and healthy.
Betta fish are known to be aggressive, especially males, so they may injure other fish when kept together. Although some people keep them with small peaceful fish, attacks can still happen. The safest option is to keep betta fish alone so they stay healthy and maintain their bright colors.
Betta fish do not actually need an aerator because they have a special organ called a labyrinth organ that allows them to breathe directly from the air. An aerator may be useful in larger tanks with other fish, but for a single betta fish in a small tank, strong water flow can cause stress. Keeping the water clean and calm without an aerator is usually enough.
